## Per-example notes

For each example, fill in a row. Keep it short — a few words per cell is enough.

- **My flags vs labels**: did you spot what the annotators marked? Anything they missed, or marked that you disagree with?
- **Span size**: is each marked span a few words, a clause, or a whole sentence?
- **Evidence**: to check the claim, is the needed info in one place in the context, spread across several places, or absent?
- **Type agrees?**: do you agree with Conflict vs Baseless?
- **Hard for NLI?**: anything a sentence-pair model would struggle with (numbers, dates, JSON fields, paraphrase, hedging like "likely", pronouns)?

## QA summary (examples 1–5)

- **Label noise in both directions, mostly misses.** Both "clean" examples (2, 5) contain unlabeled hallucinations; ex. 1 labels one "Fahrenheit" but not the identical other. Measured precision will understate the checker; hand-check a sample of false alarms in error analysis.
- **Hallucinations sit inside sentences.** Ex. 2 pt 3 mixes supported, contradicted and baseless parts in one sentence. Sentence-level scoring can give only one verdict; supports claim splitting.
- **Evidence often spans passages.** Ex. 4: two correct claims need P1 + P3 or a P1 → P2 chain. The single-passage rule would flag them.
- **…but combining passages lets blends through.** Ex. 5 step 3 stitches P1 and P2 into an instruction neither gives. Single-passage vs concatenated checking is a tradeoff to measure, not choose upfront.
- **Misattribution** (ex. 1: right content, wrong passage cited) is invisible to an "any passage" rule.
- **World knowledge needed** (ex. 3: base of skull vs base of neck), so word overlap misleads NLI.
- **Faithful ≠ true** (ex. 3 clickbait health claims, ex. 4 "Agency"). Project measures faithfulness to retrieved passages; state as a limitation.
- **Not labeled by RAGTruth:** omissions (ex. 3) and weakened claims (ex. 3 cure → alleviate).
- **Passage quality:** MS MARCO passages are scraped fragments, with broken words (ex. 2), dangling references (ex. 4) and typos (ex. 4).

## Summary-task summary (examples 6–10, reviewed by Claude; facts re-verified against the data)

- **Under-labeling gets worse with long, error-heavy responses.** Ex. 6 has 3 labels but at least 4 more clear errors. Ex. 9 misses a contradiction in its second sentence.
- **Possible annotator fatigue on long articles.** Ex. 9 (1,711 words): the note says Te Souymoy is never mentioned, but she appears at word 1,666. One example and other explanations fit (see row 9), so treat it as a hypothesis: error analysis should test whether checker–label disagreement rises with article length.
- **Source documents can contradict themselves.** Ex. 9: "Ly never saw [her mother] again" (word 102) vs the mother appearing alive (word 1,666). Need a rule for when one chunk entails a claim and another contradicts it; PROJECT.md's rule is silent on this.
- **Evidence lands in different chunks.** Ex. 6 and 9 are 1,567 and 2,159 DeBERTa tokens, with key evidence anywhere from word 230 to word 1,666. Chunking is required, and claims must be checked against *every* chunk. The short articles (ex. 7, 8, 10: 267–484 tokens) fit in one chunk, though ex. 10 is right at the limit once the claim is added.
- **Entity swaps** (ex. 6: Roberts↔Kennedy, Obergefell↔Windsor; ex. 9: mother↔sister) are the dominant Summary error. The surrounding words match, so the checker must catch *who*, not *what*.
- **Blending within one document** (ex. 8: Kerry's statement + the Twitter hashtag), the same pattern as ex. 5 across passages.
- **Strengthening goes unlabeled.** Ex. 6 "a key provision" → "the Act"; ex. 7 "According to the Kremlin, the US approached…" → stated as fact. Both make the claim stronger than the source, so by our ex. 3 rule they should count.
- **Overlapping spans carry different types** (ex. 9: same text both Baseless and Conflict). For binary detection, take the union; for the contradicted/unsupported analysis, these spans are ambiguous and should be reported separately.
- **Generator quality matters.** In this sample, GPT summaries (ex. 7, 10) were clean and Llama-2/Mistral (ex. 6, 8, 9) had nearly all the errors. That matches the dataset-wide hallucination rates, but five examples alone wouldn't show it.

## Data2txt summary (examples 11–15, reviewed by Claude; facts verified against the data)

- **Convert JSON to sentences before NLI.** Every Data2txt label here was checkable from one JSON field (`intimate: false`, `street: false`, `trendy: false`), but NLI models are trained on sentences. Converting each field ("The ambience is not intimate") turns these into the easy case. It also shrinks context: ex. 11 is 859 tokens, of which the reviews are only 386.
- **`null` means unknown.** Leave null fields out of the converted text. Writing `casual: null` as "not casual" would turn *unsupported* into *contradicted* and corrupt that split.
- **`0:0-0:0` hours are ambiguous.** 227/1,033 Data2txt sources (22%) have at least one. Across all labels whose note cites it, 38 read it as *closed*, 4 as *open 24/7* (when every day is `0:0-0:0`), 105 give no reading. Ex. 11 and 14 have reviews suggesting the business *was* open. **Decision (2026-10-20): treat as *closed*,** matching the annotators' majority reading, since the verifier is scored against their labels. State the ambiguity in the write-up. The neutral wording remains available (`zero_rule="literal"` in `src/data/linearize.py`).
- **Times need normalizing.** Ex. 13: `20:30` → "10:30 pm" was missed by annotators and needs 24h→12h conversion. Convert times to "8:30 pm" form when converting the JSON.
- **Promotional fluff is the dominant unlabeled error.** "must-visit", "popular", "excellent selection", "can get busy", "fresh produce", "friendly staff". Annotators label some ("cozy", "unique atmosphere") and miss more. Expect many checker "false alarms" here to be correct catches.
- **Hedges get dropped.** Ex. 15: "great sushi spot though I've never tried" → "delicious sushi"; "I assume" → fact. Same strengthening pattern as ex. 7.
- **Faithful to odd data.** Ex. 12 all-false Ambience, ex. 14 no takeout at a drive-thru. Faithful ≠ true applies to structured data too.
- **Generator gap holds:** GPT (ex. 12, 14) clean or 1 label; Llama-2/Mistral (ex. 11, 13, 15) carry the rest.
- **Data quirk:** 86 spans have no annotator note (`meta` is None); loader now handles it.

## Overall findings (draft by Claude — review and edit)

1. **Granularity.** Do the labeled spans line up with sentences, clauses, or smaller units? What should one "claim" be?
   - Spans range from one word ("Fahrenheit", "Roberts", "casual") to whole sentences; most are phrases or clauses. Sentences often mix supported and unsupported parts (ex. 2 pt 3, ex. 11). → One claim = one checkable fact (roughly a clause). Run the sentence-level baseline anyway, as the comparison point.
2. **Evidence location.** How often does checking a claim need more than one place in the context?
   - Usually one place, but not always: ex. 4 (two correct claims need two passages), ex. 5 and 8 (blends of two places). Long Summary articles (ex. 6, 9: 1,567–2,159 tokens) put evidence far from the start. → Check every chunk; compare single-passage vs concatenated checking, since each fails differently (ex. 4 vs ex. 5).
3. **Label quality.** How often did you disagree with the annotators? Any systematic pattern?
   - Often, and mostly in one direction: **missed hallucinations**. 7 of the 9 Llama-2/Mistral responses in the sample have clear unlabeled errors (ex. 2, 5, 6, 9, 11, 13, 15); among the 6 GPT responses, only ex. 14 ("local") does, plus borderline cases in ex. 7. Wrong *types* too (ex. 9). Labels are inconsistent in strictness ("feminist", "cozy" flagged; "must-visit", "countries" not). → Measured precision will understate the checker. Hand-check a sample of false alarms and report the fraction that are real misses.
4. **Conflict vs baseless.** Is the distinction clear in practice, or blurry?
   - Clear for Data2txt (a field says `false` vs `null`). Blurry for prose: ex. 1 "Conflict" is really misattribution, ex. 9 labels the same text as both. → Report the contradicted/unsupported confusion, but expect noise; Data2txt is the cleanest place to measure it.
5. **implicit_true.** Having seen examples, should these count as hallucinations for this project?
   - **Decision (after ex. 4):** yes, count as *unsupported*. The project checks claims against retrieved passages, and a checker cannot tell "true but not in the passages" from "invented". Also report metrics with implicit_true spans excluded, as a sensitivity check.
6. **Data2txt.** How would an NLI model check a sentence against JSON? What would need to change?
   - Convert JSON to sentences first: omit nulls, convert 24h times, decide the `0:0-0:0` rule. Treat reviews as ordinary passages. See Data2txt summary.
7. **Surprises.** Anything that changes the plan.
   - The PROJECT.md aggregation rule doesn't cover a source that contradicts itself (ex. 9): need a tie-break when one chunk entails and another contradicts.
   - Faithful ≠ true (ex. 3, 4, 12, 14): state clearly that the project measures faithfulness to retrieved text.
   - Not labeled by RAGTruth: omissions, weakened claims. Strengthened claims (dropped hedges, "key provision" → "the Act") are usually unlabeled but should count.
   - Report every metric per task. Data2txt has 65% of spans and behaves differently.
